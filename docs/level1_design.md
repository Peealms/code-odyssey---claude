# The tutorial (was Level 1) – Pixel Adventure

Status: **built 2026-11-12** - `scenes/levels/level1/level1.tscn` (main menu Play starts it;
the level id is still `level1`). Update this file when the design changes.

Level 1 is now a **one-room tutorial**: the old Room 0, where Byte teaches how the game works.
Level 1's other lessons (Rooms 1-6 and the final exam) move to the final level, in Treasure
Hunters art (`docs/cut_list.md`). Everything below **"The old seven-room Level 1"** describes
those rooms; it stays here as the reference until `docs/level2_design.md` is rewritten.

## The tutorial room
One 44 x 21 tile room, about one screen: start, Byte's gate (`System.out.println`), the spike
corridor, the Variables manual chest, Chelzy's wall-jump shaft, the fruit gallery, and on the
plateau at the top right Sarah's rescue flag and the trophy. All 16 fruit are needed for both.
The level-complete screen says TUTORIAL COMPLETE!

### What Byte teaches (`scripts/levels/tutorial.gd`)
Each talk pauses the game and shows Byte in a dialogue box (Enter moves on); the camera glides
to whatever Byte is talking about. The talks are `ByteTalk`s (`scripts/ui/byte_talk.gd`), the same base
the rescue cutscene (`hero_cutscene.gd`) uses.

| When | Byte says |
|---|---|
| The level starts | who Byte is; A/D and Space; Sarah waits at her flag and needs all the fruit; the gate opens only for code; the chest has a manual (press F) |
| The chest opens | (the chest window's tip) click the book to put it in your hotbar |
| The book is in the hotbar | which slot; press its number to hold it, left-click to read, A/D turn pages, F/Esc close |
| The book closes the first time | manuals explain the code with an example; the last page's println line opens the gate (or, if it's open already, read a manual whenever you're stuck) |
| 1st wrong line (in the terminal) | wrong lines are fine; fix the first X |
| 3rd wrong line (in the terminal) | after the room's hint: SHOW ANSWER types the line but costs a star |
| The gate opens (after the terminal closes) | your first line of Java; how to wall-jump the shaft; stamina |
| After Sarah's rescue cutscene | next level you'll switch to her with code; touch the trophy |

The terminal's `wrong_try(room, tries)` and `closed` signals exist for these talks.

## Building and testing the tutorial
- The map is generated by `tools/build_level1.gd` - a **text map**, one character per tile
  (`#` ground, `B` building blocks, `=` bar, `f` fruit, `^` spikes, `x` pit spikes, `w` wall
  spikes, `K` checkpoint, `h` chest; `s` start, `g` gate, `k` Sarah's flag, `G` trophy). Edit the
  map and re-run:
  `godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/build_level1.gd`
  (it **overwrites** level1.tscn, so don't hand-edit the scene unless you stop using the script).
  After adding a new `class_name` script, run `godot --headless --path . --import` once first.
- `tools/test_level1.gd` plays the tutorial (needs a window, not `--headless`):
  `godot --path . --script res://tools/test_level1.gd` - prints 26 PASS/FAIL checks, and saves
  screenshots of Byte's talks to the user data folder (`test_screenshots/`).

# The old seven-room Level 1 (reference for the final level)

## Building and testing the old level
- These rooms were in `tools/build_level1.gd` and `tools/test_level1.gd` until 2026-11-12; the
  old maps and their 105 checks are in git history (commit 80011e1).

## Look (matches Pixel Frog's Pixel Adventure demo)
Every room is a closed box with a frame, joined to the next by a doorway in a 2-tile wall.
The camera follows the player. Rooms have **floors**: some start at the bottom and finish at
the top, some the other way round. Simple rooms are one screen; rooms with floors are 1.5-2
screens tall; the final room is 2 screens wide and 2 tall.

| Room | Size (tiles) | Frame | Ground | Blocks | Background | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Wake up | 40 x 21 | stone | grass | bronze | green | spikes |
| 1 Wide gap | 40 x 32 | wood | orange | metal | yellow | saws |
| 2 Bridge | 40 x 21 | teal | pink | gold | purple | spiked balls |
| 3 High ledge | 40 x 32 | stone | grass | orange | blue | falling platforms |
| 4 Fan shaft | 40 x 43 | wood | orange | bronze | brown | Rock Heads (+ a saw and Spike Heads in Psalm's vents) |
| 5 Three paths | 40 x 21 | teal | pink | metal | gray | Spike Heads |
| 6 Final exam | 80 x 42 | stone | orange | gold | pink | all of them + fire |

- Floors and walls are the demo's bevelled blocks (2x2, 3-wide bars, 3-tall pillars), not bricks.
- Pits have spikes on the floor that send you to the last checkpoint.
- Walls you must *not* climb (Chelzy can wall-jump up any wall) have wall spikes that also
  send you to the checkpoint - with plain damage Chelzy could climb through them while blinking.
- Room styles live in the `ROOMS` table in `tools/build_level1.gd`.

## Fruit, rescues and traps
- **Fruit unlocks progress.** The HUD shows the current room's fruit (e.g. FRUIT 3/15).
  - Rooms 0-3: the hero you'll rescue waits (greyed out) beside the flag with a fruit counter.
    The flag only rescues them once every fruit in that room is collected.
  - Rooms 3-5: crates block the exit doorway until the room's fruit is collected. Room 3's
    crates also wait for Psalm's rescue (they say FREE PSALM): Room 4's shaft has no way back
    up, so without him you'd be stuck at its bottom. With jumpPower 2.0 you could jump over
    the flag, so the crates are the real guard.
  - Final room: the goal needs **all** the final exam's fruit; if some is missing Byte says
    where (e.g. "2 MORE FRUIT: THE LOW TUNNEL 2").
- Fruit per room: 16, 22, 20, 23, 29, 26, then 15 / 12 / 12 / 11 / 15 / 20 in the exam sections.
- **Optional corners** (still needed for the fruit): Room 0's gallery, Room 1's high bars,
  Room 2's upper floor, Room 3's falling-platform stairs to a balcony, Room 4's Rock Head
  shuttle to a balcony, the exam's shelf over the tunnel, loft over the fans and gallery
  over the falling floor. None of them lead past a puzzle.
- **Missed fruit in Room 4's drop:** the doors need every fruit, and the drop is one way
  down, so a Rock Head elevator rides between the bottom and the entrance ledge with the
  drop's bananas hanging in its path. Falling straight down or riding it picks them up, and
  a sign at the bottom says to ride it back up.
- **Rescue cutscene**: the game pauses, the camera zooms on the hero, Byte introduces them, then a
  card shows the ability on a loop, the key, the stamina cost and `hero = "...";`.
- **Backgrounds**: one full-screen background that crossfades to the room's colour as you walk in.

## Code gadgets only listen in their own room
A bridge, fan, path platform or gate only reacts to code typed while the player is in its
room (or that room's doorway). So the final exam can reuse `bridgeLength`, `fanOn` and `path`
with new values without breaking the rooms already solved - Room 2's bridge keeps 11 blocks
when you type `bridgeLength = 9;` in the exam. The exam's six sections share a **zone**, so
code typed anywhere in the exam reaches all of them (e.g. `path = 'C';` typed in the fan
corridor still makes path C appear in the next section).

## Byte fixes redeclarations
Typing `String hero = "April";` when `hero` already exists is refused (Java's rule), Byte
explains why and puts the corrected line `hero = "April";` in the input - press Enter.
This doesn't count as a wrong try.

## Goal
The player learns to **declare variables of each basic type** and to **change a variable they
already made**. Instead of fighting, the player changes the level by writing variables in
Byte's terminal. About 10–15 minutes.

## Heroes are unlocked room by room
The game starts with **only Chelzy**. Clearing a room unlocks the next hero, and the very
next room has an obstacle **only that new hero can pass** – so the player must switch with
`String hero = "...";`.

The heroes are the five members of our team, drawn in the style of the art packs
(`tools/make_team_sprites.gd` draws them, `tools/make_team_heroes.gd` animates them; each hero
only has the animations of their own ability).

| Hero | Trait | Unlocked after |
|---|---|---|
| Chelzy | Wall jump (stamina) | start |
| Sarah | Dash (stamina) | Room 0 |
| April | Double jump (stamina) | Room 1 |
| Kervin | Sprint (stamina) | Room 2 |
| Psalm | Shrink (stamina) | Room 3 |

### Psalm's Shrink (`scripts/player/player.gd`)
- Hold **Down** (S or the Down arrow) to shrink; let go to grow back. The `shrink` animation
  plays forwards to shrink and backwards to grow; tiny Psalm has `small_idle`, `small_run`,
  `small_jump` and `small_fall`.
- The hitbox goes from 16x24 to 10x12 (same feet), so he fits a **1-tile (16 px) gap** that
  stops everyone else. Tiny Psalm runs at 0.6x speed and can't swing the sword.
- Staying small drains 20 stamina a second. Under a low ceiling he can't grow back (letting go
  does nothing), the stamina bar holds still, and Byte refuses `hero = "...";` ("No room!").
  He grows as soon as the full hitbox fits, nudged up to 3 px off a wall if needed.
- Sounds: `shrink` and `grow`, two 8-bit slides made by `tools/make_shrink_sounds.gd`.

| 0 Wake-up | Start bottom-left, gate, spike corridor, shaft up, finish top-right (gallery above the corridor) | `System.out.println("Hi");` opens Byte's gate | Wall-jump shaft → **Chelzy** | Sarah |

| 4 Fan shaft | Drop to the bottom (checkpoint; a Rock Head rides back up), cross Psalm's vents to the fan room, fan up 22 tiles to the platform, Rock Head elevator to the top | `boolean fanOn = true;` | The vents, all **Psalm**: a 1-tile gap under the wall, a low vent with a saw along its ceiling (stay tiny; it sends anyone taller back to the checkpoint), a jump into a 1-tile gap over a block, a Rock Head lift, a high vent where two Spike Heads slam down to just over tiny Psalm's head, and a 1-tile gap into the fan room | – |

### The final exam (every variable gets a new value)
| Section | Code | Hero | Traps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 High wall | `jumpPower = 2.0;` (1.5 isn't enough) | April's double jump | saw, wall spikes |
| 2 Low tunnel | `jumpPower = 1.0;` (at 2.0 you hit the deadly ceiling spikes) | Psalm's shrink (halfway, a 1-tile gap under a wall with a fruit in it) | fire block, spikes |
| 3 Short bridge | `bridgeLength = 9;` | any | spiked balls, Rock Head elevator up |
| 4 Fan trap | `fanOn = false;` (the fans blow you into deadly spikes) | any | fans, ceiling spikes, saw |
| 5 Last path | `path = 'C';` | Sarah's dash (or a double jump / sprint jump) | Spike Heads slam onto the path |
| 6 Final gate | `System.out.println("I can code!");` | Kervin's sprint, then Chelzy's wall jump | falling floor, fire, saw |

Notes
- Room 2: the sign says how many blocks the gap is. Too few blocks = you fall; `5.5` = Byte
  explains int can't hold decimals.
- The exam reuses every variable, so the lesson is that a variable that already exists is
  changed with `name = value;` (writing the type again is a redeclaration error).
- The terminal follows Java here from the start: once `hero` is declared (Room 1), switching
  again in Room 2 must be `hero = "April";`. Byte explains this the first time
  ("hero already exists... leave out the type") and fills in the fixed line.
  (CodeTerminal has an "Allow Redeclare" switch if we ever want to relax this.)
- Section 5 doesn't force Sarah: an 8-tile gap is also crossed by April's double jump or
  Kervin's sprint jump. A gap only the dash crosses would need a low ceiling, and that
  stops the dash-jump too.
- Very careful tap-jumps can get through the low tunnel at jumpPower 2.0 without typing the
  code. Normal jumps can't.

## How it's built (step 1 - done)
- `VariableLibrary` lists every variable; each level's CodeTerminal picks its
  **Allowed Variables**. Gadgets listen to `CodeTerminal.variable_set(name, value)`.
- `Room` (scenes/levels/room.tscn): size, title, hint, blanks, answer.
- `Checkpoint` (scenes/objects/checkpoint.tscn): respawn point + optional **Unlock Hero**.
- `Level` (scripts/levels/level.gd): base script for every level - respawn, checkpoints,
  hero unlocks, HUD, stars. `Game.start_level / finish_level` track answers shown & mistakes.
- jumpPower scales jump **height**: 1.0 = 50 px, 1.5 = 75 px, 2.0 = 100 px.

## Gadgets (step 2 - done), all in scenes/gadgets/
| Scene | Driven by | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `byte_gate.tscn` | `System.out.println(...)` | Blocks shatter. `Required Text` = exact message, empty = anything. `Height` in blocks. |
| `code_bridge.tscn` | `int bridgeLength` | Crate blocks pop in left->right from the node (top-left of block 1). Changing the number adds/removes. |
| `code_fan.tscn` | `boolean fanOn` | Lifts the player up to `Lift Height` (default 160 px). `false` turns it off. `Start On` and `Width` (32 every 2 tiles = a wall of air). |
| `path_platform.tscn` | `char path` | Set `Letter` A/B/C; chosen letter is solid, others are see-through outlines. `Show Letter` on the first of a path. |
| `falling_platform.tscn` | - | Shakes for `Fall Delay` (0.35 s) when landed on, drops, returns after 2.5 s. One-way from below. |
| `spikes.tscn` | - | `Count` spikes in a row; rotate 180 deg for a ceiling. 20 damage. |
| `saw.tscn` | - | 20 damage. `Travel` = slide offset (shows a chain), `Speed`. |
New code-driven gadgets extend `CodeGadget` (set `variable_name`, override `_on_value`).

## Hints, "Show answer" and stars
- Every wrong line: Byte explains the failed step (as now).
- 3 wrong tries in a room: Byte gives that room's puzzle hint.
- 5 wrong tries: Byte shows the line with blanks, e.g. `int bridgeLength = __;`.
- From 3 wrong tries a **Show answer** option appears. It asks first: *"This costs a star. Show?"*
- Stars when the level is finished (best result is saved):
  - 0 answers shown → 3 stars
  - 1 answer shown → 2 stars
  - 2 or more → 1 star

## Failing
Falling or reaching 0 HP → back to the last **checkpoint flag** with full HP.
Variables already set **stay set**.

## Level complete screen
Stars, fruits collected, time, and mistakes made.

## Anti-skip rules (added 2026-10-23)
- Hero-only obstacles are inside 2-tile crawlspaces (no jumping): dash gaps are 5 tiles over a
  1-tile spike trench, falling floors need the sprint, bridges can't be jumped.
- April's double jump ignores jumpPower; Room 3's ledge needs jumpPower 2.0 + double jump.
- Tiny Psalm: the only 1-tile-high gaps in the maps are Room 4's and Exam 2's. In a 2-tile
  crawlspace he has more headroom to hop than the others, so tiny Psalm runs at 0.6x speed:
  fast enough hops would cross a falling floor before each platform drops.
- Heroes step up onto a lip of 2 px or less without jumping (`_step_onto_lip` in player.gd).
  A falling platform that has just started to sink leaves the ground beside it a hair
  higher, and Kervin could stick on that corner at the end of Room 3's falling floor.
  A real wall still stops everyone.
- Dash: 0.3 s, 90 px. `tools/test_level1.gd` tries every hero-only obstacle with the other
  heroes at jumpPower 2.0, tiny Psalm included (105 checks).
